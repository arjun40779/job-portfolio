## Problem

HorizonVision's previous site was a purchased template. Every change to a headline, a service description or the portfolio needed a developer, so the site lagged months behind the agency's actual work. The showreel and project pages were the part clients looked at most, and they were the most out of date.

## What I built

- **A content model around how the agency talks about its work.** Sanity documents for services, projects (with video, client, deliverables and credits) and pages, plus shared settings for navigation and contact details.
- **Composable page sections.** Pages are built from a small set of section blocks, such as hero, project grid, services list and call to action, which the marketing lead arranges in Sanity Studio.
- **Project pages generated from content.** Adding a project in the CMS creates its page, adds it to the portfolio grid and links it from the related service.
- **A fast, media-heavy frontend.** Next.js with statically generated pages, responsive images from Sanity's image pipeline and lazy-loaded video embeds behind poster images.

## Key decisions

**A handful of section blocks, not a free-form page builder.** A fully flexible builder would have let the site drift off-brand. Six well-designed blocks covered every page the agency needed while keeping layouts consistent.

**Video behind a poster image.** Embedding several players on load made pages heavy. Each video shows a lightweight poster first and loads the player only when someone presses play, which keeps the portfolio grid quick on mobile.

**Live preview in the Studio.** Editors see their changes rendered in the real site layout before publishing, which made the marketing lead comfortable editing without asking for a review.

## Results

- The marketing team now updates copy, services and projects in Sanity without a developer.
- New projects go from footage to a published page in the same day.
- Every page shares the same set of blocks, so the site stays consistent as it grows.
